## 3. The live interview (transcript the Interviewer handed to the Evaluator)

**Question 1** (asked 5 Nov 22:53:10, answered 22:54:52)

A: "I gave him a time because when a client is stressed, 'I'll get back to you' sounds like nothing will happen. **Question 2** (asked 5 Nov 22:54:57, answered 22:55:53)
Q: In Task 3 you wrote that you would "delete or reverse" the second GFS-3318 entry on 5 November. Which would you actually do, and why? And before changing anything, how would you make sure the 5 November entry really is a duplicate and not a second invoice?
A: "I would reverse it, not delete it. A reversing entry leaves a record of what happened and why, so if the manager or an auditor looks later they can see the mistake and the fix. Deleting it hides the history. To be honest, in my answer I wrote 'delete or reverse' because I wasn't sure what BrightBooks prefers, so I'd also check the firm's usual practice. Before changing anything I would check: the invoice number and amount on both entries are the same, the supplier statement from Gulf Fresh shows only one GFS-3318, and the bank shows only one payment. If all three agree, it's a duplicate."

**Question 3, new scenario** (asked 5 Nov 22:56:02; posted again 6 Nov 09:37:06 because no answer had arrived; answered 6 Nov 09:59:20)

A: "I wouldn't just run payroll with the QAR 4,500, and I wouldn't change it back to 3,500 on my own either, because maybe the waiter really got a raise. Then I'd contact the café owner, Mrs. Al-Kuwari, or whoever approves salaries there, and ask her to confirm the salary in writing before 2 pm. If she confirms, I run it and keep her confirmation on file. If I can't get an answer in time, I'd ask my manager whether to pay the other staff on time and hold only that one salary until it's confirmed."
